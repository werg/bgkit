# BgKIT reset — 2026-09-03

**Status: PROPOSAL, awaiting the user's decision.** Written after a full read
of the history (plans, memory, commits, registry, running containers). It
supersedes nothing yet. If accepted it supersedes
`plans/capability_packaging_2026_08_20.md` as the active direction.

The target is a **viable academic contribution**: one clean claim, one
reproducible system, one recognised benchmark protocol, with baselines the
field already trusts. Everything below is judged against that and nothing
else.

---

## 1. The honest scoreboard

After ~5 months and roughly 25 named training runs, this is what the record
supports, measured, on our own data:

| Claim | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| The encoder produces document-specific reps | base reps retrieve their own document at top-1 0.898 (better than raw token embeddings, 0.688) | **Yes** |
| The decoder uses those reps on a task the field would recognise | summarization base: rep gap −0.02 nats on its own task; BABILong 16k/32k bgkit arm: 0.00 on every cell; compaction (blob_sft_v1): probe gap 0.000; Phase-2 v10/v11: fraction of full-text information captured −8% to +4% | **No** |
| The decoder uses the reps on our self-built families | v6 fileneedle EM 0.385 → 0.026 zeroed; later found every family has a guessability/echo floor that contaminates the reading; v12 is the fourth attempt at a family whose floor is low enough to read | **Contaminated** |
| Verbatim extraction from reps | EM 0 at 67× and at 6×, and with the gold span forced to survive | **No, and accepted as architecture-bound** |
| Beats an external baseline anywhere | no bgkit number on any external benchmark exceeds the stock-model truncation arm | **No** |

There is no external, positive result. The one thing the project has proven
beyond doubt is that the *machinery* is enormous and works mechanically:
packed FA4, survivorship with dual-ascent thresholds, two levels plus bridge,
projection, splice, tool-call grammar for two chat templates, browse trees,
free-running evaluator, live config, NVMe checkpointing, three kernel forks.

## 2. How it went wrong — the patterns

None of these is any one agent's fault. They are structural, and the reset
must remove the structures, not just the symptoms.

1. **Complexity outran measurement.** The eval surface (tool-call XML/JSON
   per template, entrypoint ids, `route_exact`, `unsurfaced_id`,
   teacher-forced vs free-running, per-family floors, zeroed/full-text arms)
   is so large that *at least seven* reported numbers in three weeks turned
   out to measure something other than what they were read as. The commit
   log since 08-23 is mostly "fix: the metric read X for a reason unrelated
   to the phenomenon." When the instrument is that fragile, every run is a
   debugging run, whatever it is called.

2. **Self-built task families as the yardstick.** lognav, fileneedle,
   grepset, swerecall, reconstruct, xref: every one has needed a floor
   analysis, a decontamination pass, and a rebuild. The field's standard
   sets (NQ/TriviaQA/HotpotQA/SQuAD passages, LongBench slices, BenchPress)
   already have known floors, published baselines, and no echo problem. We
   spent the weeks the paper needed on inventing yardsticks.

3. **Phase inertia.** "We are in Phase 2, so the run uses L1" — even for
   tasks that call for one document and one level. The user's refocus asked
   for L0-only; the run lineage stayed L0→bridge→L1→projection, and the
   bridge, the L1 head, the L1 threshold, L1 retention jitter, and the L1
   selection mode each cost an investigation. Two levels is a corpus-scale
   idea; it has no business in a single-document experiment.

4. **Inherited checkpoints as ground truth.** Every Phase-2 run starts from
   the summarization round-robin step 51945. Nobody has re-derived that
   checkpoint's health from first principles, and its known properties are
   alarming (see §3). Building on it means every result carries an
   unexamined prior.

5. **Optimising the wrong end of the pipeline for weeks.** Survivor choice,
   span survival, θ controllers, head scale, bridge scale: the user already
   identified this as the recurring red herring (reps are distributed, not
   positional). The one investigation that found a real defect (rank
   collapse, interface contract) was the one that measured the
   representation directly rather than the selection.

## 3. The suspected hidden training defect — three concrete suspects

The user's fear that "we screwed up training in a way that is hard to
detect" is, on the record, well founded. Three things stand out, all of
which the literature's recipes avoid by construction:

**(a) The decoder was rewritten, not adapted.** The Phase-1 lineage trains
the Qwen3.5-0.8B decoder with full weights (no LoRA block in
`phase1_summarization_round_robin.yaml`). Result: the tied embedding matrix
sits at **row-wise cosine 0.56 to pristine** and 0.8× the row norm; plain
next-token perplexity on FineWeb-Edu is **30 vs 14 stock** before any Phase-2
run touches it. A decoder whose token geometry has been rotated that far is
not "Qwen3.5-0.8B"; it is a new model of unknown quality, and every
downstream comparison to stock is confounded by it. Every published
soft-compression method (ICAE, xRAG, COCOM, PISCO, OSCAR, BenchPress) keeps
the decoder frozen or LoRA-adapted precisely so the reps are forced onto the
decoder's existing manifold rather than the decoder being dragged to the
reps.

**(b) The reps live off-manifold by two orders of magnitude.** Survivor
vectors reach the decoder at ~200–500× the embedding norm on the base (the
bridge operates at ~500×). Step 2.5 was invented in April to repair exactly
this and was then dropped from the Phase-2 lineage. The August rank collapse
(activation scale 143 → 70985) was the same free direction, again. The
interface contract (`DecoderInterfaceNorm`) is the right idea and arrived
five months late; it should be part of the architecture from step 0, not an
opt-in.

**(c) The objectives never required the reps.** Phase 1's summarization
round-robin gives −0.02 nats rep-dependence on its own task. Phase 2's QA
families were answerable without reading (union of answer spans ≪ budget →
query-blind is optimal; 57% of loss tokens were a copy task). Compaction
continuations are predictable from the live window. A compressor trained on
objectives with a rep-free shortcut learns the shortcut. The literature's
stage-1 objective is *autoencoding plus continuation of the compressed
text*, where the shortcut does not exist — we had that in Step 3 and left it
behind.

The clean way to settle all three at once is not more probing of the old
checkpoint; it is to train the minimal system from pristine weights with the
proven recipe and see whether it behaves like the literature says it should
(reconstruction BLEU > 0.9 at 4×, meaningful QA gains at 16×). If it does,
the old lineage was the problem. If it does not, the survivorship encoder
itself is, and we will know within days rather than months.

## 4. What contribution is actually available

The soft-compression field (2023–2026) has converged: encoder + projection →
fixed number of memory slots → frozen/LoRA decoder, trained by
autoencoding + instruction tuning or distillation, evaluated on
NQ/TriviaQA/HotpotQA/SQuAD passages, LongBench slices, reconstruction BLEU,
at ratios {4…128}×. BenchPress standardises this with **Qwen3-0.6B** as a
first-class decoder. Repeating that with a new name is not a contribution.

What BgKIT has that the field does not, and that can be stated as a claim:

**Claim A — adaptive-length, ratio-conditioned soft compression.** Fixed-slot
methods train one model per ratio and emit the same slot count for a
paragraph as for a chapter. Our survivorship encoder emits a
content-adaptive number of vectors and the ratio is an inference-time knob
on a single model (dual-ascent threshold or exact top-k). If one model,
swept across {4,8,16,32,64,128}× at inference, matches or beats per-ratio
fixed-slot models at matched budget, that is a clean, sellable result. A
secondary axis in the same experiment: content-adaptive allocation across
documents at a fixed *total* budget (hard documents get more vectors), which
fixed-slot cannot express at all.

**Claim B — code.** Nobody in the compression literature evaluates on code;
one 2026 paper reports latent repo compression beating full context at
4–128× on a 3B/7B coder. CrossCodeEval has published 0.5B anchors. Our
corpus and tooling are code-native. This is the natural second experiment
once Claim A holds, and it is where the two-level/corpus machinery could
eventually re-enter, staged and gated.

**Claim C — query-conditioned compression.** Real but not now: the record
shows query-conditioning never became load-bearing because the budget was
never binding. Keep the mechanism, do not make it a claim until Claim A is
in hand and a binding-budget experiment is designed.

**Recommendation:** the paper is Claim A, evaluated on the BenchPress
protocol plus the NQ/TriviaQA/HotpotQA passage-QA axis, with Claim B as the
distinguishing second section if time allows. Headline framing: *"one
compressor, any ratio: learned token survivorship as adaptive-length soft
compression for sub-1B decoders."*

Why this is the right size: BenchPress decoders and ratios are within a
single DGX Spark; the baselines are published numbers plus one fixed-slot
model we train ourselves under the identical recipe (that ablation is the
paper); the metric is EM/F1 and BLEU with no floors to invent.

## 5. Discard, park, keep

**Discard from the critical path (leave in the repo, stop maintaining):**
- The tool-call surface: bgkit XML/JSON calls, entrypoint ids, browse trees,
  `route_exact`, `unsurfaced_id`, free-running tool execution. The paper
  splices vectors into a prompt; the decoder never emits an id.
- L1, the L0→L1 bridge, L1 head/threshold/jitter, recursive trees, cached-L0
  Stage B. Parked until Claim B needs corpus scale.
- Falcon-H1 round-robin. One decoder family.
- The self-built families (lognav, fileneedle, grepset, swerecall,
  reconstruct, xref) as *evaluation*. They can survive as a small
  code-domain training mix later; they are not yardsticks.
- Kernel tracks (Luce megakernel, FlashQLA native, DeltaNet custom kernel,
  FA4 sm12x extension). None gates the paper.
- Phase-3 SWE-bench distillation. Not reachable at 0.8B; the benchmark doc
  already says so.

**Keep (the assets that carry over):**
- The bidirectional pruned encoder, the survivorship head, exact top-k and
  the dual-ascent threshold: this *is* the method.
- The projection block, now always behind `DecoderInterfaceNorm` with the
  affine pinned.
- The interleaved splice primitive in the decoder (`forward_interleaved_with_loss`).
- Packed FA4 data path, samplers, checkpoint registry, live config, the
  rep-distinguishability probe, the splice guard, `lm_health`.
- The benchmark research (`docs/05_benchmark_targets.md`); the BABILong and
  RULER stock baselines already run.

**Retire as a starting point:** the summarization round-robin step 51945 and
every checkpoint descended from it. The reset trains from pristine weights.

## 6. The reset plan

Each milestone has a gate that is a *published-comparable number*, stated
before the run, measured by a script that exists before the run.

**M0 — Minimal system, pristine weights (≈1 week).**
- Model: L0 encoder (pruned bidirectional Qwen3.5-0.8B as now, initialised
  from pristine weights) + survivorship head + projection +
  `DecoderInterfaceNorm` (fixed affine) → **frozen pristine Qwen3.5-0.8B
  decoder with LoRA (rank 32–64)**. No L1. No bridge. No Falcon. No tool
  grammar: the prompt is `[instruction] <ctx> vectors </ctx> [question]`.
- Objective: ICAE recipe. Autoencoding (reconstruct the compressed text) +
  continuation (predict the text following the compressed span), on a
  generic mix (FineWeb-Edu already on NVMe + our code corpus), ratio sampled
  per batch from {4…128}× with continuous descent on the *minimum*.
- Gate: reconstruction BLEU at 4× > 0.9 and at 16× > 0.6 on held-out text
  (ICAE reports median BLEU > 0.98 at 4× on a 7B; we set the bar lower for
  0.8B and raise it if beaten); decoder `lm_health` within 5% of stock at
  every eval (a frozen decoder with LoRA should make this trivially true,
  which is the point). Rep-distinguishability top-1 ≥ base's 0.898.
- If the gate fails: the survivorship encoder is the defect, and the next
  experiment is a fixed-slot control under the *same* trainer to bound it.

**M1 — Instruction stage + the paper's main table (≈2 weeks).**
- Fine-tune (encoder + LoRA) on the standard passage-QA training mix
  (NQ, TriviaQA, HotpotQA with gold + retrieved passages; SQuAD) plus the
  autoencoding replay so the reps stay content-bearing.
- Eval: BenchPress suite at {4,8,16,32,64,128}× from *one* checkpoint;
  NQ/TriviaQA/HotpotQA EM/F1 vs ratio. Arms: no-context, full-context,
  truncation, LLMLingua-2 at matched ratio, and our own fixed-slot control
  trained by the identical recipe (the ablation that makes the claim).
- Gate: at 16× we retain ≥ 90% of full-context EM on NQ (OSCAR/PISCO
  territory), and the one-model ratio sweep is within noise of the per-ratio
  fixed-slot models at every ratio, or better at ≥ 2 of them.

**M2 — Code section (≈2 weeks, optional for v1 of the paper).**
- CrossCodeEval cross-file completion with the dependency files compressed
  at matched token budget vs BM25-RAG; reconstruction BLEU on code.
- This is where the two-level idea could return as a *future work* paragraph
  with one pilot number, not as infrastructure.

**M3 — Write-up (≈1 week).** Method, main table, ratio-sweep figure,
adaptive-allocation figure, honest-limits section (verbatim extraction,
aggregation tasks, query-conditioning not yet load-bearing).

Total: 6–7 weeks of disciplined work to a submittable draft. The current
trajectory has no such horizon.

## 7. Operating rules for every agent on this project

1. **One level, one decoder, one prompt format** until a gate says otherwise.
   Any config that enables L1, Falcon, or tool-call generation on the reset
   lineage needs the user's explicit instruction in the session.
2. **No new task family without a published comparable.** If a number
   cannot be placed next to a number from a paper, it is a diagnostic, not a
   result, and it does not get a run of its own.
3. **Frozen decoder.** LoRA only. If a run needs full decoder fine-tuning to
   work, that is a finding about the reps, not a knob to turn.
4. **Interface contract on from step 0**, affine pinned; rep norm, rank,
   distinguishability and `lm_health` logged every eval, from every trainer.
   These are cheap and they caught every real defect this project ever had.
5. **Gates before runs.** The gate number, the floor it must clear, and the
   script that measures it are committed before `docker compose up`.
6. **Diagnose the old lineage only if M0 fails.** No more probes of step
   51945 or its descendants; they cannot produce a paper number.
7. **Budget for debugging is one day per run.** If a run is on its third
   "fix: metric read X for a reason unrelated to…" commit, stop the run and
   simplify the instrument instead.
